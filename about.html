About us

Our story

Community MRI Project is a Boston-based nonprofit working to make MRI access more affordable and available to the people who need it. 50 years since the basic physics of magnetic resonance imaging was first published, MRI scanners remain expensive and impractical for many communities to access. Here's how the open-source MRI movement — and our work — is making them smaller and less costly:

  1. Replace superconducting magnets with permanent magnets
  2. Redesign electrical components for low power
  3. Replace specialized shielding with algorithms
  4. Simplify operator software
  5. Empower local manufacturing and maintenance

By implementing these shifts, researchers have shown that the material cost of building an operational diagnostic scanner can be slashed to as little as 1% of a conventional machine's price.

Our work is inspired by the growing open-source medical imaging movement — projects like the OSI² ONE, the first open-source MRI scanner, which showed that low-field, low-cost MRI hardware can be built from openly shared designs.
